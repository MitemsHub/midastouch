MITEMSHUB V28 — WHY 92% OF TRADES END ON THE TIMEOUT, AND WHAT EXIT WOULD HAVE CAPTURED
Date: 2026-09-13
Run: all 8 modes, M30 2024.01.01-2026.09.10, 100% real ticks, 42.4M ticks, 47,184 bars
Build: v28 with tick-true peak-excursion tracking (new)
Sample: 16,449 trades across the 8 modes

===================================================================
0. THE MEASUREMENT HAD TO BE BUILT FIRST
===================================================================
V28 recorded no excursion data at all, so the question was unanswerable from the
existing logs: a trade that times out reports where it finished, never how far in
front it ever was. Added, tick-true and always on:

  * `TrackPeakExcursion()` — every tick, before the guardian, samples
    PositionGetDouble(POSITION_PROFIT) / g_risk_money and keeps the maximum.
    Running ahead of the guardian is what makes the recorded peak the peak that
    existed, rather than the peak that survived to the close.
  * `g_peak_r` — best NON-NEGATIVE excursion of the open trade, folded with
    realised R at the close (the exit fill can itself be the best excursion),
    printed on every CLOSE line and reset per pass with the other run metrics.
  * `MFE_SUMMARY` — one line per run counting how many trades cleared each level
    of {0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.50, 2.00} R.

Every mode's captured close count equals its report's Total Trades (8/8 checked),
so the corpus below is complete, not a sample.

Measure the mean at ~0.42R and the 90th percentile at ~0.88R, in every single
mode, across entry rules that disagree on direction, trigger and filter. In R
units the favourable excursion over a 3-hour hold is therefore a property of the
instrument's volatility over that window, not of the signal: if the entries
carried directional information, these distributions would have to differ. They
do not. The tiny spread that does exist (LONG_ONLY best at ge1.0R 9.9%,
SHORT_ONLY worst at 4.0%) is the same direction as the P&L asymmetry, but on
81-181 trades it is not separable from noise.

===================================================================
2. THE 4xATR TARGET IS UNREACHABLE — TICK-TRUE, NOT INFERRED
===================================================================
    trades that ever reached 2.00R ....... 2 of 16,449   (0.012%)
    largest favourable excursion seen .... 2.005R

The spec's take-profit is 4 x H1 ATR, while the stop is 2 x H1 ATR, so the TP
sits at 2.0R by construction. Over 33 months of real ticks, in every mode, in
all 16,449 trades, two trades touched it. That is the whole explanation for
"0 TP exits / 91.7% timeouts": the timeout is not beating the TP to the exit,
the TP is simply not at a price the instrument visits — the median trade peaks
at 0.35R, i.e. 70% of the distance to the stop and 18% of the distance to the
target.

3. WHAT EXIT GEOMETRY WOULD HAVE CAPTURED (priced, not guessed)
===================================================================

MFE reached k, with all else held fixed. It is exact for this question because a
trade whose MFE reached k must have reached it while open — if it later hit the
stop, the TP would have fired first; if it timed out, the TP would already have
filled. Delta versus the run as shipped, in USD:

ANSWER: exit geometry is not the lever. No take-profit level rescues any mode
that loses; six of eight stay negative at their best level, and the two positive
modes gain at most +34% (REVERSE_BOTH, 181 trades, PF 1.02 -> 1.03) — inside
noise and far below the PF 1.30 gate. The tighter targets that help the losing
modes (0.25-0.50R) are exactly the ones that destroy the modest winners, which
is the signature of an edge-free process: capping upside helps only when the
upside was never real.

===================================================================
4. LIMITS OF THIS MEASUREMENT
===================================================================
* The counterfactual holds the entry sequence and sizing fixed. A tighter TP
  frees the one-position slot earlier, which would admit trades this run never
  took; that can only be priced with a re-run, and it is second-order against a
  deficit of -2820 to -5923 in the losing modes.
* Fills are assumed at the level with no slippage or spread widening; a
  tick-true MFE is a slightly optimistic trigger point for a real TP order.
* MFE is computed from PositionGetDouble(POSITION_PROFIT)/risk, so it inherits
  the same denominator as R and is directly comparable to it.
* MAE is not tracked, so the mirror question — whether a tighter STOP helps —
  cannot be priced from this corpus.
